# Hopecore bingo: practice sources

Research checked 6 October 2026. This collection follows the user's example: hopeful academic, design-lab and workshop language about changing systems together. Calling these sources “hopecore” is our editorial interpretation, not their self-description. The sources establish vocabulary and references; they do not establish that the practices are empty or ineffective.

Each excerpt below is exact and short. Candidate squares are editorial proposals, sometimes shortened or recombined. Use the linked pages for further reading, not as permission to reproduce their text or artwork.

## 1. Dark Matter Labs: Radical Imagination for Tomorrow

[Official project page](https://darkmatterlabs.org/feed/radical-imagination-for-tomorrow).

Excerpt: “the integration of systems thinking, future thinking and collective storytelling”.

The project connects a Seoul innovation campus to citizen capacities, relationships with nonhuman actors and equitable futures. This is a particularly close institutional counterpart to the supplied abstract: multiple methods become a route from inner change to system change.

Candidate squares: radical imagination; collective storytelling; equitable futures; non-human actors; systems thinking; portfolios of interventions.

## 2. Dark Matter Labs: Designing Our Futures

[Official project page](https://darkmatterlabs.org/feed/designing-our-futures).

Excerpt: “emerging at the intersection of the material and immaterial”.

A New European Bauhaus project connects the built environment to new institutions, accounting and investment. Its prose supplies a distinct register: social imagination meets financial and governance redesign.

Candidate squares: polycrisis; regenerative investment; systemic transition; cross-sectoral dialogue; new value flows; tangible and intangible.

## 3. Superflux: Calling for a More-Than-Human Politics

[Anab Jain's essay on the studio website](https://superflux.in/index.php/calling-for-a-more-than-human-politics/).

Excerpt: “alternate experiential futures”.

Jain discusses using a future domestic environment to make climate disruption tangible. The account explicitly combines hope, adaptation and a move beyond human-centered design. Useful as both a rhetorical source and a reference for making an imagined future feel physical.

Candidate squares: more-than-human; experiential futures; speculative artefacts; radical adaptation; making futures tangible.

## 4. Dreaming Beyond AI

[Official project site](https://dreamingbeyond.ai/en).

Excerpt: “a collective, changing body of work”.

The project gathers artists, researchers and organizers around technology, marginalization, feminism and imagination. Its stated aim includes moving technology away from the center of connection. The current site also describes care, community and embodiment as part of its Living Technologies program.

Candidate squares: collective dreaming; care; embodiment; shared values; de-centering technology; living technologies.

## 5. Joseph Rowntree Foundation: Interrogating imagination infrastructure

[Report by Ruth Potts and Keri Facer, 19 June 2025](https://www.jrf.org.uk/imagination-infrastructures/interrogating-imagination-infrastructure).

Excerpt: “maintain distinctive, diverse ways of knowing over time”.

The report examines which resources, rules and relationships support collective imagination. It is especially useful because it also questions the vocabulary: the authors report disagreement about what imagination infrastructure means, and distinguish a shortage of imagination from the suppression of particular people's imaginations.

Candidate squares: imagination infrastructure; ways of knowing; collective visions; slow attention; social ecology; unlearning.

Editorial inference: use this source to keep the joke perceptive. A bingo match indicates a recognizable register, not proof that a text lacks substance.

## 6. Collective Imagination Practices Toolkit: Layering Horizons

[Practitioner toolkit page](https://www.collectiveimagination.tools/layering-horizons-for-collective-imagination).

Excerpt: “opening and deepening reflective practices”.

This is source material for the language of facilitated reflection and layered time horizons. It connects the abstract's present/future dimensions to a practical workshop format.

Candidate squares: collective imagination; reflective practice; horizons; deepening; emerging possibilities.

## 7. Presencing Institute: Social Presencing Theater

[Official introduction](https://www.presencing.org/offerings/spt-self-paced).

Excerpt: “embodied presence, movement theater, stillness, and dialogue”.

The method uses bodily and social arts practices to perceive a present system and explore futures. The same page names tools including sensing journeys, container building and empathy walks. This is a rich source for the somatic workshop register.

Candidate squares: embodied presence; emerging future; deep sensing; holding a container; social field; empathy walk.

Editorial inference: “holding a container” is a proposed bingo label inspired by the listed Container Building tool, not a quotation from this page.

## 8. Transition Design: framework

[Framework description on the official staging site](https://staging.transitiondesign.net/about/).

Excerpt: “Vision; 2) Theories of Change; 3) Mindset & Posture; 4) New Ways of Designing”.

The framework relates future visions, assumptions about change, reflective dispositions and design practice. It is an obvious conceptual neighbor to the supplied paper's integration of speculative and systemic methods. The indexed page text was accessible in search; direct opening returned a server error during this check.

Candidate squares: theories of change; mindset and posture; transition; grassroots visions; wicked problems; new ways of being.

## 9. CAFx: Slow Down

[Organizer's exhibition account](https://www.cafx.dk/post-collection/2026-06-23-085008).

Excerpt: “new rhythms, frictions, and imaginaries”.

The first Copenhagen Architecture Biennial's main exhibition explored restraint and deceleration in architecture. Its descriptions connect quiet material gestures to changed social rhythms. It gives the project a curatorial register beyond journal abstracts.

Candidate squares: imaginaries; slowness; restraint; friction; practices of care; rhythms of change.

Editorial inference: a visual direction could borrow the contrast between serious curatorial typography and soft, tactile forms. No claim is made that this page itself uses that precise visual formula.

## 10. Danish Architecture Center: Copenhagen Architecture Biennial

[Host institution's event page](https://dac.dk/en/events/copenhagen-architecture-biennial).

Excerpt: “a slower rhythm”.

DAC describes the 2025 event through durability, nature connection and participatory encounters with materials. This is a useful second institutional voice around the same event, rather than an independent artistic lineage.

Candidate squares: participatory experience; connection with nature; durability; sensory encounter.

## 11. Daniel Christian Wahl: regenerative design

[Author's official website](https://www.danielchristianwahl.com/).

Excerpt: “Catalysing place-sourced regenerative design for human and planetary health”.

Wahl's own framing joins place, regeneration and multiple scales of health. This is compact source material for the regenerative-culture register, distinct from the speculative-method language in the user's abstract.

Candidate squares: regenerative cultures; planetary health; place-based; catalysing; living systems.

Editorial inference: “place-based” is a normalized candidate label for the site's more particular wording, not an exact quote.

## 12. Julia Watson: Lo–TEK

[Designer and author's official site](https://www.juliawatson.com/home-new).

Excerpt: “Design by Radical Indigenism”.

Watson presents Lo–TEK as a design theory connected to Indigenous knowledge and her research practice. It is a useful reference for the adjacency between ancestral technologies and future design. Do not flatten distinct Indigenous practices into a generic spiritual vocabulary.

Candidate squares: ancestral knowledge; ecological intelligence; radical indigenism; other ways of knowing.

Editorial inference: the ancestral/futures connection is our grouping; the excerpt does not use the phrase “ancestral futures”.

## 13. Adam Flynn: Solarpunk, Notes toward a manifesto

[Author's 2014 essay at Arizona State University's Project Hieroglyph](https://hieroglyph.asu.edu/2014/09/Solarpunk-notes-toward-a-manifesto/).

Excerpt: “infrastructure as a form of resistance”.

Flynn ties hopeful futures to reuse, community and material autonomy. The essay also names an Art Nouveau garden aesthetic and Miyazaki as visual associations. Those are direct source-supported references for a bright, organic visual world.

Candidate squares: solarpunk; local resilience; generativity; community; creative reuse; future generations.

Editorial inference: solarpunk is an adjacent visual and political tradition; it is not interchangeable with the workshop language being parodied.

## 14. Bayo Akomolafe: The Untimely

[Talk and transcript hosted by Long Now](https://longnow.org/talks/02026-akomolafe/).

Excerpt: “a staying with uncertainty, staying with failure”.

Akomolafe questions linear progress and prescribed futures. This is a source for poetic, relational language about time and uncertainty, with an important distinction: his argument unsettles easy improvement narratives rather than simply promising a better world.

Candidate squares: staying with uncertainty; nonlinearity; openings; temporalities; emergence; unlearning progress.

## Material to turn into the product

The strongest seed vocabulary is: collective imagination, regenerative, more-than-human, embodied, emergent, participatory, systemic, speculative, relational, co-create, care, unlearning, living systems, planetary, imaginaries, transformative, situated, dialogue, tensions, futures, community, ways of knowing, holding space, weaving and possibility. This is an editorial synthesis, not an assertion that every word occurs in each source.

For generator structure, vary the rhetorical action as well as the nouns: convene a dialogue; create a space; surface a tension; connect scales; reframe a relationship; prototype a future; cultivate a capacity. These proposed building blocks are original paraphrases of recurring moves across the corpus.

A recognizable invented sentence: “Together, we invite situated communities to weave embodied practices of care into regenerative imaginaries.” This is deliberately generated parody, not an attributed quotation.

For the paste checker, preserve the matching evidence: a square should show the phrase that triggered it in the user's text. Overlapping phrase families need restraint: one occurrence of “participatory reflection” should not silently prove five unrelated ideas. An academic abstract with many such matches can still describe rigorous work.
