# Visual language: Complexity Science Hub and Edinburgh Futures Institute

Inspected 6 October 2026: both live homepages in headless Chrome at 1440 × 1100, their public HTML/CSS, the CSH designer's case study and selected original image assets. Screenshots include cookie banners; observations concern the visible underlying pages and separately inspected assets. No app design changes are implied by this note.

## What the references actually show

**Complexity Science Hub currently combines forceful typography with flat geometry.** The live homepage has a white header, a large black hero panel, a very large white statement and isolated saturated circle, square and triangle shapes. The wordmark is tightly set and split across two lines, with a five-point asterisk-like mark between words. The website embeds Neue Haas Grotesk Text Regular and Bold. The overall impression is compact, heavy, direct and editorial. These are observations from the [current homepage](https://csh.ac.at/), not a claim about every historical campaign.

The designer explicitly describes the five-point star as a network node, a research hub or a human-centered symbol. Square, circle and triangle distinguish research fields. Category image inspection shows flat yellow and ochre circles in a simple repeated arrangement; the photographed identity banner uses pale blue, black and a dense bold headline. The inspected anniversary publication combines flat geometry with a photograph of Earth. The current evidence does **not** establish a signature CSH gradient direction or gradient hex values. [WHY Studio's CSH case study](https://why.studio/case-studies/csh/), [official category artwork](https://csh.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Societies-under-stress-768x432.png).

**Edinburgh Futures Institute separates a sober institutional frame from colorful speculative imagery.** Its current homepage uses a white background, dark text, an uppercase stacked heading, a university crest and an architectural video. Lower cards bring in soft luminous backgrounds and improbable cutout combinations. The visible examples include a turquoise field with a pale central glow, an owl on gray and astronaut bodies with green apple heads. This is not a uniformly gradient-covered website. [Current EFI homepage](https://efi.ed.ac.uk/).

The EFI stylesheet declares Source Sans Pro for headings and body text. Its named brand variables are pink `#C9464F`, orange `#F0841F`, green `#6AC59C` and blue `#4092AB`; accent is dark `#252525`, with white as a base. These are exact CSS values, not sampled estimates or a complete brand manual. The HTML also includes generic WordPress gradient presets, which should not be mistaken for EFI's authored palette. [Public EFI stylesheet](https://efi.ed.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/generatepress/style.min.css).

An official jellyfish image gives a particularly clear gradient example: a warm pale cream glow around the middle fades outward toward saturated orange at the edges. It is a broad radial field, not a diagonal two-stop stripe. Three cutout jellyfish sit against it: purple and magenta caps, with cyan, turquoise and green threads below. The large central form and smaller repeated side forms create a floating, mildly uncanny specimen display. These color names and the radial description are visual observations; no exact pixel samples were taken and the CSS brand orange should not be presented as the image's measured edge color. [Original EFI jellyfish asset](https://efi.ed.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Jellyfish1920x1200-edited-1.png).

## Why this reads as the user's hopecore

Interpretation: the shared mechanism is an authoritative institution presenting openness, relation and possibility through visual contrast. Typography supplies certainty; color and imagery supply speculative space. CSH makes complexity feel legible through elementary shapes. EFI makes futures feel strange but inviting through luminous fields and recognizable objects in unfamiliar combinations.

Useful terms for discussing the style are **institutional optimism**, **speculative collage**, **soft radial glow**, **oversized grotesk typography**, **modular geometry** and **scientific specimen imagery**. These are our descriptive labels, not names claimed by either institution.

The more specific references are Swiss-style typographic hierarchy and elementary geometric systems on the CSH side, and editorial photomontage with a faintly surreal specimen quality on the EFI side. This is a visual association, not a verified statement of either designer's influences. A generic purple-blue software gradient would miss much of what distinguishes these examples.

## Possible translation for prizes and social images

Proposal, not a redesign specification: give a prize name most of the image's visual weight, using a heavy, tightly set sans serif across a few deliberate lines. Pair it with one simple emblem and a warm cream-to-orange radial glow, or a mint-to-blue field. Keep supporting information small and readable. This combines CSH's typographic confidence with EFI's atmospheric imagery without reproducing a logo, photograph or composition.

The award itself should be the subject. A small bingo result or category can serve as context. More decoration is not automatically more dramatic: scale, a strong silhouette and a clear color field will usually carry farther when the image is reduced to a social preview.

Institution names, individual people and citations belong in this research note, not in the app's generated text or award names. The visual findings do not authorize copying either institution's brand assets.
