# Hopecore: reference map and raw material

Checked 6 October 2026. This project uses “hopecore” in the user's sense: futures-lab, workshop and academic language combining speculative methods, collective change and hopeful ecological imaginaries. The classification below is an editorial reading, not a name these authors give their work.

## The closest references

- [Dunne and Raby, Speculative Everything](https://dunneandraby.co.uk/content/bydandr/689/0): the speculative-design and social-dreaming branch. A direct connection to the supplied paper, which discusses their work.
- [Donna Haraway, Staying with the Trouble](https://www.dukeupress.edu/staying-with-the-trouble): kinship across species, sympoiesis and ways of living in damaged worlds. A strong conceptual neighbor for the ecological branch; not evidence that every futures abstract derives from her.
- [Ursula K. Le Guin, The Carrier Bag Theory of Fiction](https://www.ursulakleguin.com/the-carrier-bag-theory-of-fiction): narratives organized around gathering and sustaining instead of a heroic conquest. The direct HCI uptake below makes this more than a loose association.
- [Anna Tsing, The Mushroom at the End of the World, publisher's chapter](https://assets.press.princeton.edu/chapters/s10581.pdf): mushrooms, precarity and collaborative survival. A reference for the fungi vocabulary, with a much more particular empirical argument than that vocabulary alone conveys.
- [Arturo Escobar, Designs for the Pluriverse](https://dukeupress.edu/designs-for-the-pluriverse): relationality, autonomous design and alternative ways of making worlds.
- [María Puig de la Bellacasa, Matters of Care](https://www.upress.umn.edu/9781452953472/matters-of-care/): speculative ethics and care beyond the human.
- [Ailton Krenak, Ancestral Future, authorized excerpt](https://www.resilience.org/stories/2024-08-02/ancestral-future-excerpt/): the explicit ancestral-future association. Keep the actual argument distinct from an interchangeable slogan.

## High-yield language sources

### The user's academic example

[Systemic Futures: Integrating Critical Speculation and Systemic Design Pragmatism](https://doi.org/10.1145/3800645.3812901), Wong and colleagues (2026). The abstract supplied by the user informed the initial version; the app now uses a fictional example and includes no publication references. Its distinguishing terms include systemic, speculative, participatory, sociotechnical, grounded and critical. It describes a specific design process and case study; matching vocabulary is not a quality judgment.

### The first downloaded image

[Laura Barendregt, Futures for all: From participatory futuring to critical futurities](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid%3A16bd06a6-8c7b-44b5-aa89-26e513a0e566). The repository confirms the thesis pictured in the screenshot. Its abstract concerns who can shape futures and the politics of participation and imagination. The screenshot supplies a visual reference: condensed uppercase type, repeated FUTURE lettering, and a pink-blue textured field. The app does not reproduce the cover or social-media screenshot.

### A care workshop

[Designing with the More-than-Human: Temporalities of Thinking with Care](https://www.temporaldesign.eca.ed.ac.uk/design-for-mth-temporalities-of-care/), DIS 2023. Short source phrase: “living and once-living media”. Its workshop uses fungi, plants and insect specimens as starting points for examining temporalities and care. Candidate vocabulary: embodied; temporalities; care; imaginaries; more-than-human.

### Le Guin becomes a method

[Carrier Bag Narratives for More-than-Human Care](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802974.3807998), DIS 2026 companion publication. The workshop turns narrative approaches into practical prompts for design, maintenance and care. Candidate vocabulary: worldmaking; relational; collective; repair; co-create. This is a direct bridge between the literary reference and workshop language.

### Fabulation meets generated briefs

[Nordic Fabulation Network, Intervening via Fabulation](https://nordicfabulation.net/intervening-via-fabulation/). The organizers describe using AI-generated care design briefs to help participants enter a fabulatory mode. Short source phrase: “Caring across More-than-Human and Planetary Timescales”. Useful source for the combination of care, speculative methods, embodied intelligence and future workshops.

### A conference theme

[DRS 2026: Designing more-than-human climate futures](https://drs2026.thedrs.org/5-4-more-than-human-climate-futures). The organizers connect climate futures to plural, situated and relational approaches, aesthetics and care. Candidate vocabulary: plural; situated; relational; affect; embodied; polycrisis.

## Generator grammar

These are original compositional patterns, not source quotations:

- Question: How might [subject] help us [verb] [outcome]?
- Method: Drawing on [method] and [method], this paper situates [subject] within a wider sociotechnical ecology.
- Invitation: Join us to [verb] [outcome] through [subject].
- Workshop ritual: Bring a story, an unfinished question, or a small object that holds a possible future.
- Contribution: Our findings invite a relational approach to transformation, opening space for futures that remain deliberately unfinished.

The word bank combines academic methods with ecological and workshop vocabulary. This mixing is the parody. It should not attribute invented sentences to an author or imply that source authors endorse the app.
