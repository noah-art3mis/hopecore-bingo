# More raw material for Hopecore Bingo

Checked 6 October 2026. These ten sources add commoning, repair, postnatural studies and worldbuilding to the first collection. Excerpts are short quotations; vocabulary sets, sentence structures and samples are original editorial proposals. The proposed themes describe rhetorical neighborhoods, not interchangeable intellectual positions.

## 1. School of Commons: Visible as a Collective

[Official essay by Chantelle Lue](https://schoolofcommons.org/making-public/blog/visible-as-a-collective/).

Excerpt: “a dynamic, collectively tended space”.

The school describes its website as a digital garden maintained alongside shared learning. Documentation becomes part of how a community produces knowledge. Its extensive practice tags include commoning, radical sharing, deep hanging out and worlding.

Useful vocabulary: commoning, shared stewardship, peer learning, living archive, collective tending. Generator move: describe an ordinary object or platform as an evolving social practice.

## 2. Repair Acts: Repair Declarations

[Official project account](https://repairacts.net/all-work/repair-declarations/).

Excerpt: “co-scripting declarations with communities”.

The project gathers local statements and intentions about repair into declarations. It explicitly names maintenance-art and repair manifestos as predecessors. This supplies a credible bridge between community workshop prose and the manifesto format.

Useful vocabulary: repair cultures, maintenance, material relationships, collective commitments, restorative practice. Generator move: turn a humble maintenance action into a collective declaration, then name how people sustain it.

## 3. Design Justice Network: Principles

[The network's own principles](https://designjustice.org/read-the-principles).

Excerpt: “an accountable, accessible, and collaborative process”.

The principles organize a collective voice around who participates, who holds expertise and who experiences design's consequences. The repeated first-person plural gives a useful formal model for manifesto writing, while the content contains specific political commitments.

Useful vocabulary: lived experience, community-led, shared tools, accountable practice, facilitation. Generator move: begin parallel commitments with “We”, followed by an active verb and an identifiable object. Borrow the structure, not the network's sentences.

## 4. Arturo Escobar: Designs for the Pluriverse

[Publisher's book description and contents](https://dukeupress.edu/designs-for-the-pluriverse).

Excerpt: “Radical Interdependence, Autonomy, and the Making of Worlds”.

The publisher describes Escobar's argument for autonomous design informed by place, collaboration and the interdependence of beings. It locates this work in Latin American Indigenous and Afro-descended movements. This page establishes the book's stated scope; the full book was not inspected.

Useful vocabulary: pluriversal, autonomy, interdependence, world-making, communal, relational. Generator move: connect a local practice to a larger account of how worlds become possible. Keep specific communities and traditions out of random name substitution.

## 5. Isabelle Stengers: Introductory Notes on an Ecology of Practices

[Original journal landing page and abstract](https://epress.lib.uts.edu.au/journals/index.php/csrj/article/view/3459).

Excerpt: “coexistence and co-becoming as the habitat of practices”.

The abstract identifies the essay as a response to a proposition by Brian Massumi about political ecology and belonging. The quotation above is Massumi's proposition as quoted in Stengers's abstract. The issue is dated 2005, while the website lists a 2013 online publication date. Only the abstract was inspected.

Useful vocabulary: ecologies of practice, coexistence, co-becoming, belonging, situated practice. Generator move: treat a practice as something with surroundings and relationships, rather than an isolated technique.

## 6. Institute for Postnatural Studies: About

[The institute's own description](https://www.instituteforpostnaturalstudies.org/about/).

Excerpt: “collective learning, affective alliances, and experimental forms of knowledge production”.

IPS connects artistic research, ecological crisis, critical learning and curatorial work. Its Tender Radio program adds listening and sound to the usual visual language of futures. This is an exceptionally close match to the register the user wants to play with.

Useful vocabulary: affective alliances, planetary present, situated learning, sonic inquiry, political imagination. Generator move: assemble a triad of practices, connect them to a shared inquiry, then widen the stakes to the planet.

## 7. Serpentine: General Ecology

[Institutional program description](https://www.serpentinegalleries.org/general-ecology/).

Excerpt: “a practice of entangled interconnections and symbioses”.

The program brings ecological thinking into exhibitions, research and the organization's own operations. It crosses artistic and scientific disciplines and considers human, vegetal, animal and artificial intelligence. Useful for curatorial language that expands from an artwork to institutional transformation.

Useful vocabulary: interspecies consciousness, symbiosis, vegetal intelligence, entanglement, ecological narratives. Generator move: introduce a material encounter, then explain which assumed boundary it unsettles.

## 8. DESIGN EARTH: The Planet After Geoengineering

[Studio's Venice Biennale exhibition page](https://design-earth.org/exhibitions/planet-after-geoengineering--venice-biennale/).

Excerpt: “one planetary section from the deep underground into outer space”.

The studio uses drawings, animation and a book to explore fictional worlds after geoengineering. Its description anchors planetary scale in a specific installation and media. This provides a useful correction to purely abstract generator output: let the reader encounter an actual drawing, vessel or recording before the conceptual expansion.

Useful vocabulary: planetary section, speculative fiction, deep time, material traces, atmospheric relations. Generator move: state what is in the room, move across scale, and end with a question about inhabiting the resulting world.

## 9. Nesta: Our Futures

[Official game and download page](https://www.nesta.org.uk/feature/our-futures/).

Excerpt: “unlock public imagination about alternative futures”.

The game asks participants to invent ways of involving people in thinking about futures. Its instructions combine a public challenge, a participatory approach and emerging technologies. This is a source for the workshop register and a precedent for structured combinations that remain intelligible.

Useful vocabulary: public imagination, participatory futures, common visions, collective agency, alternative scenarios. Generator move: combine a concrete challenge, a group and a method; show what the group is invited to imagine.

## 10. Stuart Candy: Designing The Thing From The Future

[Creator's presentation and description](https://vimeo.com/111582424).

Excerpt: “a speculative design card game based on a combinatorial prompting system”.

Candy's description identifies Situation Lab's game and its underlying combinatorial structure. The linked Situation Lab page failed to open, so this entry relies on the creator's presentation description, not on third-party recreations or a watched video.

Useful vocabulary: future artefact, speculative encounter, alternative world, imagined use, situated object. Generator move: establish one future context and one object, then keep that combination consistent throughout the piece.

## Two coherent additions to the generator

### Commons & repair

Sources: School of Commons, Repair Acts, Design Justice Network, with Escobar and Stengers as conceptual neighbors.

Nouns and noun phrases: neighborhood commons; shared tools; repair circles; collective stewardship; living archives; everyday maintenance; mutual obligations; communal infrastructures; situated knowledge; cultures of reciprocity.

Actions: tend; mend; share; convene; redistribute; document; sustain; return; make room for; care for.

Concrete anchors: a borrowed ladder; a repaired kettle; a public kitchen; a neighborhood tool library; a communal table; a mended banner.

Useful tensions: access and ownership; care and exhaustion; continuity and change; shared ambition and unequal labor.

Original sentence moves:

- “At the scale of [place], [practice] becomes a way to sustain [shared resource].”
- “Through [method] and [method], participants negotiate what it means to care for [anchor].”
- “The work asks who maintains [resource], who can enter, and whose time makes it possible.”
- “We commit to [action] [anchor], and to keeping the terms of that commitment open to revision.”

### Postnatural worlds

Sources: IPS, Serpentine, DESIGN EARTH, with Nesta and Situation Lab informing structured worldbuilding.

Nouns and noun phrases: sonic ecologies; multispecies encounters; affective alliances; material entanglements; vegetal intelligence; planetary intimacy; relational responsibility; porous boundaries; speculative habitats; ecological imaginaries.

Actions: attune; listen; inhabit; compose; trace; encounter; reimagine; gather; unsettle; make perceptible.

Concrete anchors: a clay listening vessel; a recording of tidal water; a seed archive; a weathered membrane; a table of mineral fragments; a woven shelter.

Useful tensions: nature and infrastructure; intimacy and planetary scale; listening and extraction; human intention and other forms of agency.

Original sentence moves:

- “[Anchor] becomes a meeting point for [living participant], [material] and [human practice].”
- “Moving between [near scale] and [far scale], the work traces [relationship].”
- “Visitors are invited to [sensory action], attending to the relations that usually remain outside the frame.”
- “What forms of [collective possibility] become imaginable when [assumption] is suspended?”

These lists are editorial constructions, not extracted quotations. Keep each paragraph inside one thematic vocabulary so the prose has a recognizable subject rather than a pile of unrelated terms.

## New format: manifesto

Use a short title, one sentence naming the present condition, three to five parallel commitments, and a final invitation. Prefer declarative verbs to strings of abstract nouns. Carry one chosen subject through all commitments. The manifesto should sound collectively authored and earnest, with the joke emerging from the escalation in scale.

Original example:

**A charter for the unfinished commons**

Every shared future begins with something somebody has agreed to maintain.

We gather around the communal table as a place of reciprocal learning.

We make repair a public practice and leave room for knowledge that arrives through use.

We tend the relationships that keep our tools, stories and disagreements in circulation.

We carry these commitments into a living archive, open to those who will revise them after us.

Come with one broken object and a willingness to stay for the washing up.

## New format: exhibition wall text

Use a title and optional materials line, then two or three paragraphs: physical encounter; research or conceptual turn; visitor invitation. Establish a specific object at the beginning and return to it at the end. Keep invented dates, artist names and institutions out unless the UI explicitly labels them fiction.

Original example:

**A vessel for shared weather**

Clay, field recordings, gathered water.

A clay vessel rests at the center of the room. Its surface holds traces of touch, while recordings of tidal water move through the surrounding space.

The installation considers listening as a form of relational responsibility. Moving between bodily attention and planetary processes, it asks how sonic ecologies might reshape our understanding of a shared habitat.

Visitors are invited to pause beside the vessel and attend to the worlds already passing through it.

## Implementation guidance from the material

Choose a concrete anchor once per output and reuse it. Select two or three related concepts, a method and a tension, then let each format arrange them differently. Manifestos need commitments; wall texts need an encounter; abstracts need a question, method and claimed contribution. Merely adding a title to the same paragraph will not produce distinct formats.

A low-intensity setting can emphasize the anchor and plain actions. A high-intensity setting can expand the scale and introduce denser relationships while retaining the same subject. This makes the exaggeration readable and gives the reader something to recognize beneath the language.
